About This Project

Into a Snap is a rhythm game from the early 2000s. The game's main character, Paunch, is on a journey to learn how to snap his fingers, and he seeks help from his friends throughout the game. Paunch's friend guides you through the different styles to help Paunch progress and learn how to snap his fingers. I was the lead game designer for the project, handled the initial mock-up and designed the combo system.

The goal for the project was to create an interactive adventure for the player to learn to rely on those close to them and learn how to snap their fingers. The team initially aimed for five levels, each level was to introduce each of the friends, and on the last level, take to the streets and snap their fingers. Due to time constraints, we were able to polish out the first level and make the second level playable.

My Process & Contributions

At the beginning of the design of the project, we began as a team brainstorming ideas for the game mechanics and the game's overall theme. We wanted to create a rhythm level where the player interacts with the music and timing. I began by researching similar games, with what we were trying to achieve with the first level, with the setting being the early 2000s. One of the references I took inspiration from is Guitar Hero, with the gameplay mechanic of pressing keys at perfect timing and holding down keys for a specific duration to achieve the highest score. From this research, I created a mock-up of how we could adapt these mechanics into our game and presented it to the team for feedback and discussion. Which they were very receptive to the idea and agreed it would fit well within our game mechanics and began design of the first level.

With these mock-ups, I was responsible for the onboarding and overall level sequence for the player by providing a safe space for the player to first interact with these elements and the overall mechanics without affecting their health. This allowed the player to understand the gameplay mechanics and further increase the difficulty through the rest of the level. I mocked up how to introduce each note throughout the level to playtest and ensure the player is likely to understand and improve upon with more complicated combinations of notes. We conducted various playtests of our game to ensure our gameplay loop within the first level was balanced and that playtesters understood the system we created. From the playtest result, we notice that the blue note, which you hold down, was not clear at first how long to hold, so as a fix, we placed highlighted blue sections after the blue notes to ensure the player understands how long to press and hold the key without them suffering health loss.

Results & Takeaways

Overall, my contribution was significant in researching prior games that could provide inspiration and inform our own gameplay for the first level. Ensuring there is a space for onboarding the player to become familiar with our mechanics and significantly reducing the learning effort. Additionally, resolving any issues with the system through constant playtest data and understanding how to utilize the data to improve the first level further. As the high school year came to a close, we wrapped up by polishing the first level and some design on the second. Still, we presented at the SIA (Urban Arts) Arcade Showcase 2023, where we discussed the design process and showcased our work to parents, staff, and industry professionals. The day was a resounding success, a testament to our hard work and dedication. However, later in the year, we were given an additional opportunity to showcase our game at the Unity Student Showcase 2023 and display it at the Harlem Museum of Science for two weeks as part of their game section the following year.
