About This Project

//repla

In Gloom you play as John Ra, a man who lives in an old, run-down apartment complex. A couple of months after moving in, he’s felt as if something was lurking in the shadows, always watching him, but brushes it off as nothing. One day, as John goes to check on the mail, his elderly neighbor, Albert, asks him if he could retrieve a borrowed item from your upstairs neighbor.

Heading upstairs, you notice that something in the apartment is off. Your neighbor is nowhere to be found, but mannequins are scattered around the apartment, with one mannequin laid out on a table that seems to slowly twitch.

Traversing your apartment complex, manage your sanity. As your sanity decreases, things around you start to warp and change as apparitions of a dark creature stalk you. Heal yourself and clear your mind by taking your medication and staying in the light to regain your sanity. Are you afraid of the dark, and can you escape the creature within?

On the Gloom project, a team game project, I was responsible for designing and implementing core gameplay mechanics using Unreal Engine 5.6 and Unreal Engine 5's Blueprint system. In addition, setting up the Perforce server on my PC to gain experience using an industry-standard version control system for collaborative game design. I developed the fixed camera system used throughout the game, either in open areas or on doors, to provide an immersive experience similar to older horror game titles like Resident Evil and Silent Hill, the inventory UI, and created the interaction system with lock doors and inventory items, level design for 2 of the three levels, interfaces for the different game states, such as the pause menu and game over screen, and optimizing various gameplay elements to ensure smooth performance.

My Process & Contributions

My workflow began with extensive research into classic survival horror games to understand their mechanics and atmosphere. I started by sketching out ideas for the fixed-camera system, ensuring it would enhance the horror experience. Using Unreal Engine's Blueprint system, I prototyped these mechanics, iterating based on team feedback. One of the main challenges was creating a seamless fixed-camera system that could adapt to different environments and be mounted on doors. To overcome this, I began researching the Unreal Engine documentation and community forums to find the optimal design solution for the camera to work with the door interactions, either as a child actor of the doors or by placing the camera on the doors themselves. This involved prototyping different approaches and testing them to ensure they met the project's requirements. The children's-actor approach did work as intended, which allow my to create two child actor component which inherit from the BP_FixedCamera can generate two to use on either side of the door. Additionally, began setting trigger volumes to switch between cameras as the player navigated through the levels, ensuring smooth transitions and maintaining immersion, and keeping track of which camera is active and switching the camera when the player uses the door again.

Results & Takeaways

This project significantly enhanced my skills in Unreal Engine 5 and Blueprint scripting, particularly in developing complex gameplay mechanics. The fixed-camera system added a nostalgic yet effective layer of horror to the game, which was well-received during playtesting. I learned the importance of iterative design and team collaboration in game design. The experience with Perforce also provided valuable insights into version control practices, essential for any game design project. Overall, Gloom was a rewarding experience that honed my technical skills and deepened my understanding of survival horror game design.
